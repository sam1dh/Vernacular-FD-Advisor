## You can always break an FD early

Indian banks must allow premature withdrawal of a term deposit at the request of the depositor, per the RBI's Fair Practices Code. "Premature withdrawal" means closing the FD before the chosen tenor ends — in Hindi, "FD ko beech mein todna". Money is never fully locked; the cost is a lower effective interest earned.

## How the penalty is calculated

When you break an FD early, the bank pays interest at the rate applicable for the period the money actually stayed deposited, minus a penalty (commonly 0.50% to 1.00%). Example: you booked a 12-month FD at 8.50% p.a. but broke it after 6 months. If the bank's 6-month rate is 7.50%, you get 7.50% − 0.50% penalty = 7.00% p.a. for those 6 months, not the 8.50% you booked.

## No interest for very short holdings

If a cumulative FD is closed before the minimum tenor (often 7 days for banks), no interest may be payable at all. Banks publish minimum tenor rules in their deposit terms.

## Partial premature withdrawal

Some banks allow partial premature withdrawal — breaking only part of the FD while the rest continues at the original booked rate and tenor. This avoids losing interest on the full amount. Whether partial withdrawal is allowed depends on the bank's terms; many Small Finance Banks permit it once per FD.

## After maturity

If you do not give instructions at maturity, banks typically auto-renew the FD for the same tenor at the prevailing rate. RBI rules require banks to inform the depositor of maturity; many banks now credit maturity proceeds to the linked savings account if instructed at booking time.
