## Two ways to receive interest

A non-cumulative (payout) FD pays interest to you at regular intervals — monthly, quarterly or annually — while the principal stays intact until maturity. A cumulative (reinvestment) FD does not pay anything during the tenor; interest is added back to the deposit and the full amount is paid at maturity. In Hindi: "har mahine kamai chahiye to payout FD, poori rakam end mein chahiye to cumulative FD".

## Who should pick payout

Choose monthly or quarterly payout if you need the FD for regular household income — common for retirees replacing a salary. The payout amount equals the simple interest for the period: ₹1,00,000 at 8.50% p.a. pays ₹2,125 per month (approximately, monthly payout often uses simple interest divided by 12).

## Who should pick cumulative

Choose cumulative if you do not need the money during the tenor: it earns more because of compounding, and you do not have to reinvest each payout manually. The same ₹1,00,000 at 8.50% p.a. quarterly grows to about ₹1,08,839 in one year.

## Yield optics

Banks often advertise the effective annualised yield of cumulative FDs, which is higher than the quoted p.a. rate. When comparing two banks, always compare the quoted p.a. rate and the compounding frequency, or compute the maturity amount for both.

## Switching and breaking

The payout option is chosen at booking and usually cannot be changed mid-tenor. Premature withdrawal rules apply to both types identically: the rate for the actual period held minus the penalty applies.
