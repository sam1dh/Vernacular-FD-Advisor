## What is a fixed deposit

A fixed deposit (FD) is an agreement where you deposit a lump sum with a bank for a fixed period (tenor, for example 12 months) and the bank pays you interest at a rate agreed at the start. The rate does not change during the tenor. In Hindi: "aap paisa bank mein lock karte ho, aur bank aapko pakka kamai deti hai". The amount you start with is the principal; the extra money you receive is interest.

## What "p.a." means

"p.a." stands for "per annum", meaning per year. When a bank advertises 8.50% p.a., it means the deposit earns 8.50% interest for one full year. If you lock money for only 6 months, you earn roughly half of the annual rate for that period (actual bank rules for part-period interest apply). The rate is applied to the principal you deposited.

## Minimum and maximum deposit

Each bank sets its own minimum (for example ₹1,000 at many Small Finance Banks) and often a maximum for the advertised rate slab. Rates differ by tenor bucket: 7–45 days, 46–90 days, 91 days–1 year, above 1 year, and so on. The advertised "best rate" usually applies to one specific tenor bucket only, commonly 12 months.

## Interest payout vs reinvestment

Non-cumulative FDs pay interest out to you monthly, quarterly or annually — useful if you want regular income ("har mahine kamai"). Cumulative FDs (reinvestment) add the interest back to the principal, so the final maturity amount is larger. Banks quote the annualised effective yield for cumulative deposits, which looks higher than the headline p.a. rate because of compounding.

## Premature withdrawal

Most banks allow you to break the FD before the tenor ends, but they charge a penalty, typically 0.50% to 1.00% lower than the rate applicable for the period the money actually stayed with the bank. See the dedicated premature-withdrawal note for details.
