## Extra 0.50% is the market norm

Most Indian banks pay senior citizens (age 60+) an additional 0.50% p.a. over the regular FD rate for the same tenor. Some banks extend a higher bonus (0.60%–0.75%) or a super-senior slabs (80+ extra) at their discretion; RBI rules do not fix the quantum, banks announce it in their rate cards.

## How it changes maturity

The bonus applies to the same tenor bucket. Example: a bank pays 8.50% p.a. for a 12-month FD; a senior citizen books the same FD at 9.00% p.a. On ₹1,00,000 compounded quarterly for one year, the senior earns about ₹1,09,308 versus ₹1,08,839 — roughly ₹469 more per lakh.

## Age counted at booking

The senior citizen rate applies based on age at the time of booking the deposit (primary account holder's age). For joint accounts, the rate usually follows the age of the first account holder; some banks apply it if either holder qualifies — check the bank's terms.

## Usually only for resident individuals

The senior citizen bonus generally applies to resident individual depositors only; deposits held by HUFs, firms and companies do not get the bonus even if the signatory is a senior citizen. NRI deposits are typically excluded from the extra rate.
