## FD interest is fully taxable

Interest earned on fixed deposits is taxed as income from other sources, added to your total income and taxed at your income-tax slab rate. There is no separate special rate for FD interest. In Hindi: "FD ki kamai par bhi tax lagta hai, aapki salary slab ke hisaab se".

## TDS: when the bank cuts tax at source

Banks deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) under Section 194A when total interest from that bank exceeds the threshold in a financial year. From 1 April 2025, the threshold is ₹50,000 for general customers and ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens per bank per year (raised by the Finance Act 2025 from the earlier ₹40,000/₹50,000). TDS is deducted at 10% if PAN is linked; without PAN, 20%.

## Form 15G and 15H

If your total income is below the taxable limit, you can submit Form 15G (Form 15H for senior citizens aged 60+) to the bank each financial year, and the bank will not deduct TDS on your interest. This only stops deduction at source; if your income later turns out to be taxable, you still owe the tax when filing your return.

## Different banks, separate limits

The ₹50,000 (or ₹1,00,000) TDS threshold applies per bank, per PAN. Depositing in several banks means each bank applies its own threshold. Interest from cooperative banks and all branches of one bank are counted together for that bank.

## Reporting in the tax return

Even if TDS was deducted, you must report FD interest in your annual return. TDS already deducted appears in Form 26AS and is credited against your final tax liability.
