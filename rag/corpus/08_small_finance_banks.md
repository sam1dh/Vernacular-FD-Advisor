## What a Small Finance Bank is

Small Finance Banks (SFBs) — for example Suryoday, Ujjivan, Jana, Equitas and Utkarsh — are scheduled commercial banks licensed by the Reserve Bank of India. They are banks in the full legal sense, not NBFCs or microfinance companies. They were created to serve small borrowers and depositors who are underserved by large banks.

## Same regulation, same insurance

SFBs are regulated by RBI exactly like other commercial banks: they follow RBI's exposure norms, capital rules and deposit regulations. Deposits with SFBs are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, same as any large bank. So a ₹5 lakh FD in an SFB carries the same insurance protection as in a big bank.

## Why their rates are higher

SFBs advertise higher FD rates (often 0.50%–1.50% above large banks) because they lend to smaller borrowers at higher yields and want deposit growth. The higher rate is a business model feature, not a sign of illegality — but rate-chasing beyond the insured limit increases your exposure to that single bank.

## Practical safety rule

Because insurance stops at ₹5 lakh per bank, the common rule of thumb is: keep the total (principal + interest) with any single bank within ₹5 lakh, and spread larger amounts across different banks. Within the insured limit, an SFB deposit is as protected as a public-sector bank deposit.
