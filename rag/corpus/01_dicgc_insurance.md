## What DICGC insurance covers

Deposits in banks insured by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) are protected up to ₹5 lakh per depositor, per bank, in the same right and capacity. This limit of ₹5,00,000 includes both the principal amount and the interest earned on it. So if you deposit ₹4,50,000 and it earns ₹30,000 interest, the full ₹4,80,000 is insured because it stays within the ₹5 lakh limit. In Hindi, this is called "बीमा सुरक्षा" — your paisa is protected even if the bank fails.

## Which banks are covered

All commercial banks including public sector banks, private banks, foreign bank branches in India, regional rural banks, cooperative banks and Small Finance Banks are required to be DICGC members. Small Finance Banks like Suryoday, Ujjivan, Jana, Equitas and Utkarsh are scheduled commercial banks regulated by the RBI, so deposits held there are insured up to ₹5 lakh exactly like deposits in large banks. NBFCs, chit funds and companies accepting deposits are NOT covered by DICGC.

## What is not covered

Deposits above ₹5 lakh per bank are not insured; money in the same bank held in different branches still counts as one depositor for the ₹5 lakh limit, but different ownership types (single account, joint account) count separately. Deposits of foreign governments, inter-bank deposits and funds received outside India are not covered.

## When you get paid

If a bank is placed under moratorium or cancellation of licence, DICGC pays insured deposits within 90 days. Since the Deposit Insurance Act amendment of 2021, depositors do not need to file a claim — payment is processed through the bank's records directly. After the July 2024 amendment, DICGC must also pay an interim amount within 45 days of moratorium.

## Interest after bank failure

Only interest accrued up to the date the bank's licence is cancelled or moratorium is declared is counted inside the ₹5 lakh insurance limit. Interest accrued after that date is not insured.
